The Shelves

Six tales from six corners of the world

Prometheus Steals the Fire

Greek

A Titan defies Zeus to give humankind the gift of fire, and pays for it on a lonely mountain.

Author: Hesiod

In the first days, humans shivered in caves and ate their meat raw. The Titan Prometheus, whose name means "forethought", pitied them. Zeus had hidden fire away, and he had forbidden anyone to give it to mortals.

Prometheus crept to Olympus, lit a hollow fennel stalk at the chariot of the sun, and carried the spark down to the world. Forges glowed, bread baked, and the first cities lit their windows against the night.

Zeus's anger shook the sky. He chained Prometheus to a crag in the Caucasus, where an eagle came each day to eat his liver. Each night it grew back. The Titan did not beg for mercy, and in time the hero Heracles broke his chains.

Ragnarök, the Twilight of the Gods

Norse

Winter without end, a wolf that swallows the sun, and a last battle that ends the world to begin it again.

Author: Snorri Sturluson

First comes Fimbulwinter: three winters with no summer between them. Brothers kill brothers, and the wolves Sköll and Hati catch the sun and the moon and swallow them. The stars go out.

The chained wolf Fenrir breaks free, and the serpent Jörmungandr rises from the sea. The ship Naglfar sails with the dead, and the fire giants cross the burning bridge Bifröst.

Odin falls to Fenrir, and Thor slays the serpent but dies of its venom. Fire swallows the world and the earth sinks. Then a green land rises from the water, and two humans, Líf and Lífþrasir, walk out of hiding to begin again.

Osiris and the Eternal Night

Egyptian

A murdered king is gathered piece by piece by his devoted wife and becomes lord of the underworld.

Author: Plutarch

Osiris ruled Egypt with kindness and taught his people to farm. His brother Set, jealous of the crown, tricked him into a beautifully carved chest, sealed it, and threw it into the Nile.

His wife Isis searched the world for him. When she found the body, Set tore it into pieces and scattered them across the land. Isis gathered each piece, and with her sister Nephthys she wrapped the body in linen, the first mummy.

She breathed life into him long enough to conceive their son Horus. Osiris descended to rule the Duat, the realm of the dead, where he weighs every heart against a feather. Horus grew up and defeated Set, and the Nile flooded each year in his father's honor.

Amaterasu in the Cave

Japanese

The sun goddess hides from the world, and the other gods must find a way to coax her back into the light.

Author: Ō no Yasumaro (Kojiki)

After her storm-god brother Susanoo wrecked her rice fields and hurled a flayed horse into her weaving hall, Amaterasu fled into a rock cave and sealed it with a stone. The world went dark and the spirits of mischief ran loose.

The gods gathered outside. They hung a mirror and jewels on a tree, and the goddess Ame-no-Uzume danced on an overturned tub until the gods roared with laughter.

Amaterasu, curious, opened the stone a crack. Her own face shone back from the mirror. The god of strength pulled the stone away, and light returned to the world. A sacred rope was stretched across the cave so she could never leave again.

Quetzalcoatl and the Fifth Sun

Aztec

The feathered serpent descends into the land of the dead to recover the bones from which humanity is made.

Author: Bernardino de Sahagún

Four suns had lit and ruined the world before. After the last flood, there were no people left. Quetzalcoatl, the feathered serpent, went down to Mictlan, the land of the dead, to ask its lord for the bones of the old humans.

The lord of the dead set him tests. He gave Quetzalcoatl a conch with no holes to blow, and the god had worms bore holes in it and bees fill it with sound. Then the lord tried to keep the bones, and Quetzalcoatl fled with them. He fell, and the bones broke, which is why people are of different heights.

He carried the fragments to the goddess Cihuacoatl, who ground them like corn. The gods bled onto the meal, and humanity was born under the Fifth Sun.

Vasilisa and Baba Yaga

Slavic

A girl walks into the forest to fetch fire from a witch whose hut stands on chicken legs.

Author: Alexander Afanasyev

Vasilisa's cruel stepmother sent her into the forest to borrow light from Baba Yaga. Vasilisa carried a little doll that her dead mother had given her, which fed on crumbs and gave good advice.

The witch's hut turned on chicken legs, and its fence was made of bones topped with glowing skulls. Baba Yaga gave her impossible tasks: sort poppy seeds from dirt, clean the house, cook a feast. The doll did the work overnight.

Baba Yaga, who could not bear a blessing under her roof, sent the girl away with a skull full of burning coals. Vasilisa carried it home, and when the stepmother and her daughters looked into its eyes, the fire burned them to ash.
